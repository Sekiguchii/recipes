Chocolate Banana Walnut Bread

A deeply chocolatey banana loaf, made with butter, milk, and plenty of chopped walnuts. No yogurt, sour cream, chocolate chips, or hot water.

Your reconstructed version, adapted from Sally’s Baking Addiction. Butter and sugar are creamed first; the loaf uses milk instead of yogurt and walnuts instead of chocolate chips.

20 minPrep
60–65 minBake
23 × 13 cmLoaf tin
~10Slices

Ingredients

Chocolate banana walnut loaf

Three large bananas should give roughly 345 g once peeled. There is no hot water, yogurt, sour cream, or chocolate chips in this version.

Method

  1. Prepare the oven and tin

    Preheat the oven to 175–177°C conventional / 160°C fan. Grease and line a 23 × 13 cm (9 × 5 inch) loaf tin with baking parchment.

  2. Whisk the dry ingredients

    In a bowl, whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, baking powder, and salt. Break up any cocoa lumps.

  3. Cream butter and sugar

    Beat the softened butter and sugar together for 2–3 minutes until paler and fluffy.

  4. Add the eggs

    Beat in the eggs one at a time, mixing well after each addition.

  5. Add banana, vanilla, and milk

    Mix in the vanilla and mashed bananas, followed by the 60 ml milk. The mixture may look a little uneven; that is fine.

  6. Combine gently

    Add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture and fold together until just combined. Do not overmix.

  7. Fold in the walnuts

    Gently fold through the 100 g chopped walnuts. Transfer the batter to the prepared tin and smooth the top.

  8. Bake

    Bake for approximately 60–65 minutes, checking from 55 minutes. If the top darkens too quickly, cover loosely with foil. A skewer inserted into the centre should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter.

  9. Cool and slice

    Allow to cool in the tin for 15–20 minutes, then lift out and cool on a wire rack before slicing.

Notes for your next bake

  • Use very ripe bananas. Spotted bananas mash easily and give a sweeter, softer loaf.
  • Keep the butter soft, not melted. Creaming it with the sugar creates the fluffy base you used before.
  • Mix gently once the flour goes in. This helps keep the crumb tender.
  • Start checking at 55 minutes. Oven and tin differences can shift the baking time.
  • Cool before slicing. The loaf sets as it cools and slices more neatly.
